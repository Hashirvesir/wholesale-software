§1

Overview

Invenco runs on three kinds of records: parties (customers, vendors, and cargo/transport parties), products held as stock inside one or more warehouses, and a chart of accounts that every transaction eventually posts to. A Sale or a Purchase is the form you fill in; underneath it, the system writes proper double-entry ledger entries that everything else — Party statements, Trial Balance, Profit & Loss, Aging — is calculated from.

Nothing in this system is "just a number saved on the invoice." Every rupee that moves is traceable back to a ledger entry with a date, an account, and — where relevant — a party.

§2

The Ledger

Every transaction — a Sale, a Purchase, a Payment, an Expense, a Stock Transfer — writes one or more rows to a single ledger_entries table. Each row has an account, a debit or a credit amount (never both), a date, and, when it concerns a customer, vendor, or cargo party, a party. Rows always come in matched pairs (or groups) so that total debits equal total credits for the transaction — that balance is what Trial Balance checks across the whole system.

Dr — Debit Cr — Credit

Reading direction depends on the account type, same as any set of books:

  • Asset accounts (Cash, Bank, Inventory, Accounts Receivable) — a debit increases the balance, a credit decreases it.
  • Liability accounts (Accounts Payable) — a credit increases what's owed, a debit reduces it.
  • Income accounts (Sales) grow on credit; Expense accounts (COGS, Cargo Expense, and every operating expense category) grow on debit.

The accounts that Sales and Purchases post to by name: Cash, Bank (and any other payment account), Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable, Sales, COGS, Inventory, and Cargo Expense.

§3

Sales

A Sale is created against one customer, one warehouse (the stock source), a list of products with quantity and price, an optional flat discount, and payment — split across as many accounts as needed (part Cash, part Bank, and so on).

Creating a sale

Pick the customer & warehouse

Search selects the customer and shows their running balance right there in the dropdown, so you see who owes what before you even add an item.

Add line items

Each row is a product, quantity, and price against the selected warehouse's live stock — the same product can't be added twice at the same price, and quantity can't exceed what the form allows.

Split the payment (optional)

Add as many payment rows as needed — Cash, Bank, any configured account — each with its own amount. Leave it empty and the sale posts entirely on credit.

Save

Stock is reduced immediately from the chosen warehouse, and the ledger entries below are written in the same transaction — either all of them succeed, or none do.

What gets posted

For a sale of 5,000 where 3,000 is collected split across Cash and Bank, and the stock cost (average cost) of the goods sold is 2,100:

Sale — goods leave, revenue is recognised
DrAccounts Receivable customer5,000
CrSales5,000
DrCOGS2,100
CrInventory2,100
Payment — one pair per split, collected at sale time
DrCash1,800
CrAccounts Receivable customer1,800
DrBank1,200
CrAccounts Receivable customer1,200

The remaining 2,000 stays on the customer's Accounts Receivable balance — that's the "credit" your Party Ledger and Aging Report are reading.

Walk-In Customer

One customer and one warehouse can be marked as the defaults in Settings → Walk-In Customer Defaults. When that customer is selected, the Sale form pre-fills both fields automatically (nothing to search for on a quick counter sale), and enforces one rule that doesn't apply to anyone else: the sale must be paid in full — split across as many accounts as you like, but the total can't leave a balance due. This is checked in the form and again on the server, so it can't be skipped.

Sharing an invoice

Every Sale can be sent on WhatsApp directly from its page — the message text is a fully editable template (Settings → WhatsApp Message Templates) with placeholders for the customer name, invoice number, totals, previous balance, and a link to the invoice PDF. That PDF link is a signed URL: it opens without logging in, but the signature means the ID can't be swapped for someone else's invoice.

§4

Purchases

A Purchase works the same shape as a Sale in reverse — a vendor, a destination warehouse, line items with quantity and price (in PKR or RMB, converted at the exchange rate entered), and split payment. Stock increases immediately, and each item's average cost is recalculated on the spot using the standard weighted-average formula:

new avg cost= ( (old qty × old avg cost) + (added qty × price) ) ÷ new total qty
Purchase — goods arrive, vendor is owed
DrInventory10,000
CrAccounts Payable vendor10,000

If part of the bill is paid immediately, that split is posted the same way a Sale's payment is — Debit the payment account, Credit Accounts Payable — reducing what's owed to the vendor.

§5

Cargo & Landed Cost

Freight paid to a separate cargo/transport party can be added to a Purchase in two ways, and the system treats them differently on purpose:

Per-item cargo

A cargo amount entered against a specific line item is capitalized — folded straight into that item's average cost, the same way the goods price is. A line of 10 units at 100 with 50 cargo lands at 105/unit, permanently, until the next purchase changes the average again.

Per-item cargo — one landed-cost debit, split across two payables
DrInventory goods + cargo1,050
CrAccounts Payable vendor1,000
CrAccounts Payable cargo party50
Balanced

There's deliberately no separate "Cargo Expense" line here — the cargo cost is already inside the Inventory debit above. Adding a second expense entry for the same rupees would double-count it and throw Debit and Credit out of balance, which is exactly what used to happen before this was fixed.

Transport-mode cargo

Cargo billed as total weight × rate per kg isn't split across individual items — it's tracked as its own CargoCharge record and, in the ledger, as a genuine period expense.

Transport-mode cargo — its own expense line
DrCargo Expense200
CrAccounts Payable cargo party200

For reporting, Profit & Loss still treats this as landed cost — see the note under Profit & Loss for how it's reconciled back into Cost of Goods Sold even though the ledger keeps it in its own account.

Returning goods bought with cargo

When items from a per-item-cargo purchase are sent back through a Purchase Return, the cost removed from Inventory is the same landed cost the item came in at — goods price plus its cargo share, not goods price alone. Using goods price alone would remove less value than was actually capitalized, artificially inflating the average cost of whatever's left in stock, and understating Cost of Goods Sold on Profit & Loss for every sale after it.

§6

Financial Statements

Read the underlying ledger directly — no report-specific shortcuts, so these always tie back to the entries above.

Account Ledger

Reports → Account Ledger

"What moved through this one account, and what's its running balance?"

Every ledger entry for a chosen Chart of Accounts account, in date order, with a running balance and the contra-account each line was paired with — including split-payment lines, matched by amount so multi-account payments show the correct counterpart, not just the first match.

date rangewarehouse

Party Ledger

Reports → Party Ledger

"What does this customer or vendor's statement look like?"

The same running-balance statement, scoped to one party instead of one account — opening balance carried forward, every entry in the period, and a closing balance in Dr / Cr. Shareable straight to WhatsApp as a signed PDF link.

partydate range

Trial Balance

Reports → Trial Balance

"Does everything actually balance, as of today?"

Every account's net Debit or Credit balance as of a single date, including opening balances carried in from party records. If total Debit ≠ total Credit here, something upstream broke double-entry — it shouldn't ever happen.

as-of date

Profit & Loss

Reports → Profit & Loss

"What did we actually make, after the true cost of the goods we sold?"

Gross Profit is Sales less Cost of Goods Sold, where COGS is landed cost — goods price plus cargo, for both cargo modes. Per-item cargo is already inside the purchase cost it reads; transport-mode cargo is added back in proportionally by value even though it's booked to its own ledger account, and that account is then excluded from Operating Expenses below the line so it's never counted twice.

COGS = Opening Stock Value + Net Purchases − Closing Stock Value
  Opening/Closing Stock Value = WAC stock valuation as of that date (landed cost)
  Net Purchases = (Purchases + Cargo) − Purchase Returns + mid-period Opening Stock

Net Sales    = Total Sales − Sales Returns
Gross Profit = Net Sales − COGS

Total Income  = Gross Profit + Other Income (non-Sales income accounts)
Total Expense = Other Operating Expenses (Purchase / COGS / Cargo Expense / Return
                accounts excluded — already folded into COGS above)
Net Profit    = Total Income − Total Expense
date rangewarehouse

Balance Sheet

Reports → Balance Sheet

"What do we own, what do we owe, as of today?"

Total Assets against total Liabilities as of a single date, with Equity computed as the difference — not its own ledger account, just what's left over.

as-of date

Collections & Aging

Receivable / Payable Aging

Reports → Aging

"Who's overdue, and by how long?"

Every party's outstanding balance, bucketed by age into 0-30 / 31-60 / 61-90 / 90+ days. Reads real ledger balances — including opening-balance-only dues and cargo-party payables that have no invoice of their own — and ages them against invoices oldest-first, so the totals always tie back to the Party Ledger exactly.

as-of date

Daily Collection

Reports → Daily Collection

"How much cash and bank actually moved today?"

Every ledger entry against a payment account (Cash, Bank, and the like) in the period — received vs. paid, per account and in full detail. Picks up split-payment lines from Sales and Purchases automatically, since they post to the same accounts.

§1

Overview

Invenco teen tarah ke records par chalta hai: parties (customers, vendors, aur cargo/transport parties), products jo stock ke tor par ek ya zyada warehouses mein rakhe jate hain, aur ek chart of accounts jahan har transaction aakhir mein post hoti hai. Sale ya Purchase wo form hai jo aap bharte hain; iske peeche system asal double-entry ledger entries likhta hai, jin se baaki sab kuch — Party statements, Trial Balance, Profit & Loss, Aging — calculate hota hai.

Is system mein kuch bhi "sirf invoice par likha hua number" nahi hai. Jo bhi rupya move hota hai, wo ek ledger entry tak trace ho sakta hai — date, account, aur (agar relevant ho) party ke sath.

§2

The Ledger

Har transaction — Sale, Purchase, Payment, Expense, Stock Transfer — ek hi ledger_entries table mein ek ya zyada rows likhta hai. Har row ka ek account hota hai, ek debit ya credit amount (kabhi dono nahi), ek date, aur agar wo kisi customer, vendor, ya cargo party se related ho to ek party. Rows hamesha matched pairs (ya groups) mein aati hain taake total debit = total credit rahe — yehi balance Trial Balance poore system mein check karta hai.

Dr — Debit Cr — Credit

Reading direction account type par depend karti hai, jaisa kisi bhi accounting books mein hota hai:

  • Asset accounts (Cash, Bank, Inventory, Accounts Receivable) — debit balance badhata hai, credit ghata deta hai.
  • Liability accounts (Accounts Payable) — credit se jo owe karte hain wo badhta hai, debit se kam hota hai.
  • Income accounts (Sales) credit se badhte hain; Expense accounts (COGS, Cargo Expense, aur har operating expense category) debit se badhte hain.

Jo accounts Sales aur Purchases apne naam se post karte hain: Cash, Bank (aur koi bhi doosra payment account), Accounts Receivable, Accounts Payable, Sales, COGS, Inventory, aur Cargo Expense.

§3

Sales

Sale ek customer, ek warehouse (stock source), products ki list (quantity aur price ke sath), ek optional flat discount, aur payment — jitne accounts mein chahen utna split (kuch Cash, kuch Bank, waghera) — ke against banti hai.

Sale kaise banti hai

Customer aur warehouse select karein

Search se customer select hota hai aur unka running balance dropdown mein hi dikh jata hai, taake item add karne se pehle hi pata chal jaye kaun kitna owe karta hai.

Line items add karein

Har row ek product, quantity, aur price hoti hai — selected warehouse ke live stock ke against. Same product same price par dobara add nahi ho sakta, aur quantity form ki limit se zyada nahi ho sakti.

Payment split karein (optional)

Jitni chahen payment rows add karein — Cash, Bank, koi bhi configured account — har ek apna amount. Khali chhod dein to sale poori tarah credit par chali jayegi.

Save karein

Stock foran chosen warehouse se kam ho jata hai, aur neeche wali ledger entries usi transaction mein likhi jati hain — ya to sab successful hoti hain, ya koi nahi.

Kya post hota hai

5,000 ki sale ke liye jahan 3,000 Cash aur Bank mein split ho kar collect hota hai, aur becha gaya stock ki cost (average cost) 2,100 hai:

Sale — maal jata hai, revenue record hoti hai
DrAccounts Receivable customer5,000
CrSales5,000
DrCOGS2,100
CrInventory2,100
Payment — har split ka apna pair, sale ke waqt collect hui
DrCash1,800
CrAccounts Receivable customer1,800
DrBank1,200
CrAccounts Receivable customer1,200

Baaki 2,000 customer ke Accounts Receivable balance par reh jata hai — yehi "credit" hai jo aapki Party Ledger aur Aging Report dikhati hain.

Walk-In Customer

Ek customer aur ek warehouse ko Settings → Walk-In Customer Defaults mein default mark kiya ja sakta hai. Jab wo customer select ho, to Sale form dono fields automatically bhar deta hai (quick counter sale ke liye kuch search karne ki zaroorat nahi), aur ek rule enforce karta hai jo kisi aur par apply nahi hota: sale poori tarah paid honi chahiye — jitne accounts mein chahen split karein, lekin total mein koi balance due nahi reh sakta. Ye form mein aur phir dobara server par check hota hai, taake skip na ho sake.

Invoice share karna

Har Sale WhatsApp par seedha uski page se bheji ja sakti hai — message text poora editable template hai (Settings → WhatsApp Message Templates) jisme customer name, invoice number, totals, previous balance, aur invoice PDF ka link ke liye placeholders hain. Wo PDF link ek signed URL hai: bina login khulta hai, lekin signature ki wajah se ID kisi aur invoice se badla nahi ja sakta.

§4

Purchases

Purchase Sale jaisi hi shape mein ulta kaam karti hai — ek vendor, ek destination warehouse, line items quantity aur price ke sath (PKR ya RMB mein, entered exchange rate par convert), aur split payment. Stock foran badhta hai, aur har item ki average cost foran standard weighted-average formula se recalculate hoti hai:

naya avg cost= ( (purani qty × purani avg cost) + (add hui qty × price) ) ÷ nayi total qty
Purchase — maal aata hai, vendor ko paisay dene hain
DrInventory10,000
CrAccounts Payable vendor10,000

Agar bill ka kuch hissa foran paid ho, to wo split usi tarah post hota hai jaise Sale ki payment — payment account Debit, Accounts Payable Credit — jisse vendor ko jitna owe karte hain wo kam ho jata hai.

§5

Cargo & Landed Cost

Cargo/transport party ko diya gaya freight Purchase mein do tareekon se add ho sakta hai, aur system unhe jaan boojh kar alag treat karta hai:

Per-item cargo

Kisi specific line item ke against enter kiya gaya cargo amount capitalize hota hai — us item ki average cost mein seedha shamil ho jata hai, bilkul jaisa goods ki price hoti hai. 10 units @ 100 ki line jisme 50 cargo ho, wo 105/unit par land karti hai — permanently, jab tak agli purchase average ko dobara na badle.

Per-item cargo — ek hi landed-cost debit, do payables mein baant kar
DrInventory goods + cargo1,050
CrAccounts Payable vendor1,000
CrAccounts Payable cargo party50
Balanced

Yahan jaan boojh kar koi alag "Cargo Expense" line nahi hai — cargo cost pehle se Inventory wale debit ke andar hai. Usi rupay ke liye doosri expense entry banane se ye dobara count ho jata aur Debit/Credit balance se bahar chala jata — yehi exact issue tha jo is fix se pehle hota tha.

Transport-mode cargo

Cargo jo total weight × rate per kg ke tor par bill hoti hai, wo individual items mein baanti nahi jati — ye apna alag CargoCharge record ke tor par track hoti hai, aur ledger mein ek asal period expense ke tor par.

Transport-mode cargo — apni alag expense line
DrCargo Expense200
CrAccounts Payable cargo party200

Reporting ke liye, Profit & Loss ise ab bhi landed cost hi treat karta hai — dekhein Profit & Loss ke neeche wala note, ke ye Cost of Goods Sold mein wapas kaise shamil hota hai, chahe ledger ise apne alag account mein hi rakhe.

Cargo wala maal wapas karna (Purchase Return)

Jab per-item-cargo wali purchase ka maal Purchase Return ke through wapas jata hai, to Inventory se jo cost hataya jata hai wo wahi landed cost hai jis par wo item aaya tha — goods price plus uska cargo share, sirf goods price nahi. Sirf goods price use karne se asal capitalize hui value se kam value remove hoti, jis se bache hue stock ka average cost artificially badh jata, aur uske baad har sale ka Cost of Goods Sold Profit & Loss mein kam dikhta.

Profit & Loss

Reports → Profit & Loss

"Hum ne asal mein kitna kamaya, becha gaya maal ki asal cost ke baad?"

Gross Profit = Sales minus Cost of Goods Sold, jahan COGS landed cost hai — goods price plus cargo, dono cargo modes ke liye. Per-item cargo pehle se us purchase cost ke andar hai jo ye padhta hai; transport-mode cargo proportion se wapas add hoti hai chahe wo apne alag ledger account mein booked ho, aur wo account phir Operating Expenses se exclude ho jata hai taake dobara count na ho.

COGS = Opening Stock Value + Net Purchases − Closing Stock Value
  Opening/Closing Stock Value = us date tak WAC stock valuation (landed cost)
  Net Purchases = (Purchases + Cargo) − Purchase Returns + mid-period Opening Stock

Net Sales    = Total Sales − Sales Returns
Gross Profit = Net Sales − COGS

Total Income  = Gross Profit + Other Income (non-Sales income accounts)
Total Expense = Baaki Operating Expenses (Purchase / COGS / Cargo Expense / Return
                accounts yahan exclude — wo upar COGS mein already shamil hain)
Net Profit    = Total Income − Total Expense
